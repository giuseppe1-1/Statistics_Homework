Giuseppe Signorelli · Matricola 2327983

Each penetration attempt gets a score between 0 and 10 from a fixed distribution with mean μ and variance σ². Each of m hackers attacks n systems; Welford’s recurrences give the hacker’s sample mean and variance, and the m results are averaged and compared with μ and σ².

1Model and parameters

The score of one attempt is a random variable X in [0, 10] with a fixed distribution (chosen below). Hacker j attacks n systems and produces the trajectory x1, …, xn; Welford’s recurrences (Exercise 1) give its sample mean x̄j and sample variance sj² = SS/(n−1) in one pass. Trajectories are nested: the values for n = 10 are those of the first 10 attempts of the same hacker’s longer run.

2Welford in action: a few hackers

Running sample mean and running sample variance of the first six hackers as the number of attempts k grows. The mean settles on μ and the variance on σ² (dashed), and the spread between hackers shrinks. The shaded guide for the mean is μ ± 2σ/√k.

3Average of the sampling means and variances vs μ and σ²

For every n, the table averages the m sample means and the m sample variances (computed with Welford) and compares them with the theoretical values. The ± is the standard error of the average (sd across hackers / √m), and “z” is the difference in standard errors: values within about ±2 are compatible with equality. The last columns show what happens if the sum of squares is divided by n instead of n−1.

5Variability as n increases

Standard deviation, across the m hackers, of the sample means and of the sample variances, compared with the theory: sd(x̄) = σ/√n and sd(s²) = √[(μ4 − (n−3)/(n−1) σ4)/n], where μ4 is the fourth central moment of the scores.
